================================================================================
DATASET: MS MARCO v2.1 (microsoft/ms_marco)
USED IN: Stage 4 — Fine-tuning LexBERT for passage ranking
PURPOSE: Teach the model to rank relevant passages above irrelevant ones.
         Each query has multiple passages labeled RELEVANT (is_selected=1)
         or NOT RELEVANT (is_selected=0). The model learns pairwise ranking.
SOURCE:  Real Bing search queries with human-annotated relevant passages
         from web documents.
================================================================================

Each example shows a real user query followed by all its candidate passages.
RELEVANT passages are ones human annotators marked as answering the query.
The model is trained to assign higher scores to RELEVANT passages.

Training used 200,000 pairwise triplets (query, positive_passage, negative_passage)
with temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss over a (B, 2B) score matrix.

Query: what is a corporation?

  [RELEVANT    ] A corporation is a company or group of people authorized to act
                 as a single entity (legally a person) and recognized as such in law.
                 Early incorporated entities were established by charter. Most
                 jurisdictions now allow the creation of new corporations through
                 registration.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The word "corporation" derives from corpus, the Latin word for body,
                 or a "body of people." By the time of Justinian, Roman law recognized
                 a range of corporate entities under the names universitas, corpus or
                 collegium.

  [NOT RELEVANT] A corporation sole is a legal entity consisting of a single
                 ("sole") incorporated office, occupied by a single ("sole") natural
                 person. This allows corporations (typically churches or governments)
                 to pass without a succession of authority, property, rights and
                 duties to another person when the original holder of the office dies
                 or is otherwise removed.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Corporate governance is the collection of mechanisms, processes and
                 relations used by various parties to control and to operate
                 corporations. Governance structures and principles identify the
                 distribution of rights and responsibilities among different
                 participants in the corporation.

  [NOT RELEVANT] A cooperative differs from a corporation in that it has members, not
                 shareholders, and they share decision-making authority. Cooperatives
                 are typically classified as either consumer cooperatives or worker
                 cooperatives.

Query: why did rachel carson write an obligation to endure

  [RELEVANT    ] Rachel Carson wrote "An Obligation to Endure" (Chapter 2 of Silent
                 Spring, 1962) to argue that humans have a responsibility to protect
                 the natural environment from chemical pesticides. Carson believed that
                 since humans had introduced synthetic chemicals into the environment,
                 they had an obligation to understand and mitigate the harm caused.
                 The chapter challenges the chemical industry's claim that pesticides
                 are safe and necessary.

  [RELEVANT    ] Carson's central argument was that life has existed and adapted for
                 millions of years, but the modern age of synthetic chemicals has
                 introduced change at a pace too rapid for living things to adapt.
                 She wrote that unlike natural hazards, man-made chemicals represent
                 a threat that living organisms have had no time to adjust to, making
                 human intervention both the cause of and potential solution to
                 environmental damage.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Silent Spring is an environmental science book by American biologist
                 Rachel Carson. The book was published on 27 September 1962, documenting
                 the adverse environmental effects caused by the indiscriminate use of
                 pesticides. Carson accused the chemical industry of spreading
                 disinformation, and public officials of accepting the industry's
                 marketing claims unquestioningly.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Rachel Louise Carson (May 27, 1907 – April 14, 1964) was an American
                 marine biologist, author, and conservationist whose book Silent Spring
                 and other writings are credited with advancing the global environmental
                 movement. Carson was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of
                 Freedom by President Jimmy Carter.

  [NOT RELEVANT] DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) is a colorless, tasteless, and
                 almost odorless crystalline chemical compound that was originally
                 developed as an insecticide. It was used widely during World War II
                 to control malaria and typhus among civilians and troops.

Query: symptoms of a dying mouse

  [RELEVANT    ] Signs that a mouse may be dying include: lethargy and lack of
                 movement, labored or rapid breathing, loss of appetite and weight
                 loss, hunched posture, ruffled fur, discharge from the eyes or nose,
                 and loss of balance or coordination. A dying mouse may also become
                 cold to the touch as its body temperature drops, and may isolate
                 itself in a corner of its enclosure away from other mice.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Mice are small rodents that are found in nearly every country and
                 type of terrain. They are known to invade homes and other buildings
                 in search of food and shelter. A mouse can squeeze through a gap
                 as small as a dime.

  [NOT RELEVANT] House mice can be destructive in agricultural areas. They consume
                 and contaminate food meant for humans, livestock, or other animals.
                 They also cause structural damage by gnawing through walls, floors,
                 and electrical wires.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Rodenticides are pesticides that kill rodents. They are commonly
                 used to control rat and mouse populations. Most rodenticides work
                 by causing internal bleeding, though some affect the nervous system.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Mice used in laboratory research are often specially bred strains.
                 They are valuable in biomedical research because they share
                 approximately 85% of protein-coding regions with humans.

Query: average number of lightning strikes per day

  [RELEVANT    ] On average, lightning strikes the Earth about 100 times per second,
                 which equates to approximately 8 million strikes per day. This
                 estimate comes from satellite-based lightning detection systems
                 operated by NASA. About 70-80% of lightning strikes occur in the
                 tropics, where warm, moist air creates the convective activity
                 needed to produce thunderstorms.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Lightning is a naturally occurring electrostatic discharge during
                 which two electrically charged regions, both in the atmosphere or
                 with one on the ground, temporarily equalize their charges.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The United States experiences about 25 million lightning strikes
                 per year, or roughly 70,000 per day. Florida is known as the
                 "lightning capital" of the United States due to its high frequency
                 of thunderstorms.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Thunder is the sound caused by the rapid expansion of air surrounding
                 and heated by a lightning discharge. Because light travels faster
                 than sound, lightning is seen before thunder is heard.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Ball lightning is an unexplained atmospheric electrical phenomenon.
                 The term refers to reports of luminescent spherical objects that
                 vary from pea-sized to several metres in diameter. It is usually
                 associated with thunderstorms, but unlike lightning, it persists
                 for much longer.

Query: what causes high white blood cell count

  [RELEVANT    ] A high white blood cell count (leukocytosis) can be caused by many
                 conditions, including infection (bacterial, viral, or fungal),
                 inflammation, immune system disorders, bone marrow disorders, and
                 certain medications such as corticosteroids. In some cases, it may
                 indicate leukemia or other blood cancers. The normal white blood
                 cell count is between 4,500 and 11,000 cells per microliter of blood.

  [RELEVANT    ] White blood cells (WBCs), also called leukocytes, are a component
                 of blood that helps the body fight infection and other diseases. When
                 the body detects a foreign substance or pathogen, it increases WBC
                 production to combat the threat. Stress, both physical (surgery,
                 injury) and emotional, can also temporarily elevate WBC counts.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Red blood cells (RBCs), also called erythrocytes, are the most
                 common type of blood cell and the vertebrate's principal means of
                 delivering oxygen to the body tissues via blood flow through the
                 circulatory system.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Blood is a body fluid in humans and other animals that delivers
                 necessary substances such as nutrients and oxygen to the cells and
                 transports metabolic waste products away from those same cells.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Platelets, also called thrombocytes, are a component of blood whose
                 function is to react to bleeding from blood vessel injury by
                 clumping, thereby initiating a blood clot.

Query: how does photosynthesis work

  [RELEVANT    ] Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some
                 bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose.
                 It occurs in two stages: the light-dependent reactions in the
                 thylakoid membranes, where sunlight splits water molecules to
                 release oxygen and create ATP and NADPH, and the light-independent
                 reactions (Calvin cycle) in the stroma, where CO2 is fixed into
                 glucose using the ATP and NADPH produced in the first stage.

  [RELEVANT    ] The overall equation for photosynthesis is:
                 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2
                 Plants absorb sunlight using the pigment chlorophyll, which is
                 located in organelles called chloroplasts. The green color of most
                 plant leaves comes from chlorophyll, which absorbs red and blue
                 light but reflects green light.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Cellular respiration is the process by which biological fuels are
                 oxidized in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor such as
                 oxygen to produce large amounts of energy, to drive the bulk
                 production of ATP.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Chlorophyll is any of several related green pigments found in the
                 mesosomes of cyanobacteria and in the chloroplasts of algae and
                 plants. Its name is derived from the Greek words for pale green and
                 leaf.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Stomata are pores found in the epidermis of leaves, stems, and
                 other organs that facilitate gas exchange. The pore is formed by a
                 pair of specialized parenchyma cells known as guard cells.

Query: what is the boiling point of water in celsius

  [RELEVANT    ] The boiling point of water at standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm
                 or 101.325 kPa) is 100 degrees Celsius (212 degrees Fahrenheit or
                 373.15 Kelvin). At higher altitudes where atmospheric pressure is
                 lower, water boils at temperatures below 100°C. For example, at
                 the top of Mount Everest (elevation ~8,849 m), water boils at
                 approximately 70°C due to the significantly reduced air pressure.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The melting point of water (ice) is 0 degrees Celsius at standard
                 atmospheric pressure. Water is unusual among common substances in
                 that its solid form (ice) is less dense than its liquid form.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The specific heat capacity of water is 4,182 J/(kg·K), one of the
                 highest of any common substance. This means water requires more
                 energy to heat up than most other materials, making it useful as
                 a coolant and heat storage medium.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Steam is water in the gas phase. At sea level, steam is produced
                 when water reaches its boiling point of 100°C. Steam has many
                 industrial applications, including driving turbines in power plants.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Heavy water (deuterium oxide, D2O) is a form of water that contains
                 the hydrogen isotope deuterium rather than ordinary hydrogen. Its
                 boiling point is slightly higher than ordinary water, at 101.4°C.

Query: when was the eiffel tower built

  [RELEVANT    ] The Eiffel Tower was built from 1887 to 1889 as the centerpiece of
                 the 1889 World's Fair (Exposition Universelle), which celebrated the
                 centennial of the French Revolution. It was designed by Gustave
                 Eiffel's engineering company and was the world's tallest man-made
                 structure for 41 years until the Chrysler Building was completed in
                 New York City in 1930. Construction took 2 years, 2 months, and 5 days.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de
                 Mars in Paris, France. It is named after the engineer Gustave Eiffel,
                 whose company designed and built the tower. Locally nicknamed "La
                 dame de fer" (French for "Iron Lady"), it was constructed as a
                 temporary exhibit.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The Chrysler Building is an Art Deco skyscraper in Midtown Manhattan,
                 New York City, standing at 319 m (1,047 ft). At its completion in
                 1930, it was the world's tallest building for approximately 11 months
                 before it was surpassed by the Empire State Building.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The 1889 World's Fair (Exposition Universelle) was a world's fair
                 held in Paris, France, from May 6 to October 31, 1889. It was
                 organized to celebrate the centennial of the storming of the Bastille,
                 a seminal event of the French Revolution.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Iron is a chemical element with symbol Fe and atomic number 26. It
                 is a metal that belongs to the first transition series and group 8
                 of the periodic table. It is the most common element by mass forming
                 the Earth's core.

Query: how long does it take for food to digest

  [RELEVANT    ] The time it takes for food to move through your entire digestive
                 system — from eating to elimination — is typically 24 to 72 hours.
                 Food spends 2 to 4 hours in the stomach being broken down into
                 chyme. It then moves to the small intestine for 2 to 6 hours where
                 most nutrient absorption occurs. The remaining material spends 10
                 to 59 hours in the large intestine before elimination.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The digestive system is a group of organs working together to convert
                 food into energy and basic nutrients to feed the entire body. Food
                 passes through a long tube inside the body known as the alimentary
                 canal or the gastrointestinal tract (GI tract).

  [NOT RELEVANT] Enzymes play a crucial role in digestion by breaking down complex
                 food molecules into simpler ones. Amylase in saliva begins digesting
                 carbohydrates in the mouth. Pepsin in the stomach breaks down
                 proteins, and lipase breaks down fats in the small intestine.

  [NOT RELEVANT] The liver is an organ with a wide range of functions, including
                 detoxification of various metabolites, synthesis of proteins, and
                 production of biochemicals necessary for digestion and growth.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Fiber is a type of carbohydrate that the body can't digest. Though
                 most carbohydrates are broken down into sugar molecules, fiber
                 cannot be broken down into sugar molecules, and instead it passes
                 through the body undigested.

Query: what is the speed of light

  [RELEVANT    ] The speed of light in a vacuum is exactly 299,792,458 metres per
                 second (approximately 3×10^8 m/s or 186,282 miles per second).
                 This value was fixed by the International Bureau of Weights and
                 Measures in 1983 and forms the basis for the definition of the
                 metre. The symbol c represents the speed of light, derived from
                 the Latin word "celeritas" meaning swiftness. No object with mass
                 can reach or exceed the speed of light.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Albert Einstein's special theory of relativity, published in 1905,
                 established that the speed of light in a vacuum is the same for all
                 observers regardless of their relative motion or the motion of the
                 light source.

  [NOT RELEVANT] A light-year is the distance that light travels in one Julian year
                 in a vacuum, which is approximately 9.461 trillion kilometres
                 (5.879 trillion miles). The light-year is used to measure distances
                 in astronomy.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Radio waves are a type of electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths
                 in the electromagnetic spectrum longer than infrared light. Like all
                 electromagnetic waves, they travel at the speed of light.

  [NOT RELEVANT] Optical fiber uses light to transmit data at very high speeds.
                 However, light traveling through fiber optic cables moves at about
                 two-thirds the speed of light in a vacuum due to the refractive
                 index of the glass or plastic fiber material.
